Dumas Family Recipes

Pressed: a paper-ground cookbook with an olive spine. Publication discipline, family voice.

The idea in one line

Recipes that have been cooked more than once, set like a printed cookbook rather than a food blog. Warm paper, one serif for voice, one grotesque for machinery, and a single olive that means this is the thing you selected.

The seal

Single ring, two dots on the horizontal axis, “Dumas Family Recipes” arched over the top and running left to right along the base, and a Newsreader D struck through by the ruled DUMAS bar. Drawn on a 200-unit square grid: ring radius 97, the D at 128 with its baseline at 138, and the bar rules, the DUMAS word and the two dots all aligned to the D’s optical centre at y 93 rather than the circle’s. The band knocks through the D — where the bar crosses the letter the artwork is transparent and the ground shows through, which is why the seal only ever sits on a flat colour.

  • Four files ship with this guide: seal.svg (ink), seal-reversed.svg (paper, for the olive band), and seal-mark.svg / seal-mark-reversed.svg, the compact ring-and-D lockup.
  • Full seal at 40px and above. Below 40px — favicon, app icon, inline byline — use the compact mark.
  • Clear space on all sides equals the width of the D. Minimum full-seal size 40px, minimum mark size 16px.
  • Never recolour beyond ink and paper, never place the seal on a photograph, never stretch the ring or reset the ring text.

Photography

Our own photos only. Daylight, one light direction, plates and boards rather than styling props. Two crops: 4:3 for grid cards, 4:5 vertical for the recipe hero, which is the crop that travels well to Pinterest. 1px #DED7C7 border on every image, no radius, no overlay. If a recipe has no photo yet, use the hatched placeholder and ship it — a missing photo is better than a stock one.

Voice

Plain, specific, slightly dry. State the trick and why it works. No exclamation marks, no “delicious”, no origin stories that exist to fill space. Headnotes are two sentences. Method steps tell the cook what to look for, not just what to do: the skin should be deep amber and release without sticking — if it sticks, it isn't ready.
